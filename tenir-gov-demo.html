Scene 3 · HOLD Lifecycle

When evidence is insufficient, TENIR doesn't deny or blindly proceed. It HOLDs — a designed suspension of agency under pressure. HOLD is TENIR's signature concept.

HOLD vs QUARANTINE_LOCK

HOLD

Per-operation suspension. The action is not denied — it's pending evidence or review. A timer (τ_K) counts down.

QUARANTINE_LOCK

Per-entity lock. Blocks ALL admission and dispatch for an entire LEI until authorized release. Distinct from HOLD.

Key Doctrine: ESCALATED is Absorbing

ESCALATED has no time-based auto-exit. It remains until an authorized human signs an exit receipt. τ_K expiry in ESCALATED produces UNKNOWN, not resolution (v7.1 §19). This is deliberate — escalation requires human judgment, not a timer.

Scene 4 · In-Memory vs Persistent Registry

The default SDK client uses an in-memory registry. Idempotency claims are lost on restart. With a persistent store_path, they survive.

In-Memory (default)

DualKernelCompositionClient()

No store_path → registry lives in process memory only.

⚠ After restart: same operation re-adjudicated successfully. Idempotency claim lost!

Persistent (store_path)

DualKernelCompositionClient(store_path="ledger.json")

Store file on disk → claims survive process restart.

✓ After restart: re-adjudication blocked (HARD_VETO). Idempotency preserved!

Scene 5 · Provider Timeout → UNKNOWN → Reconciliation

When the provider times out, TENIR doesn't assume the payment failed. It marks the operation UNKNOWN and triggers reconciliation. A timeout does NOT prove no effect occurred.

Why Not FAILED?

Per doctrine v7.1: a timeout does not prove no effect occurred. The payment may have succeeded — we just didn't get the response. Treating this as FAILED could lead to a double-charge (retry a payment that already went through). UNKNOWN triggers reconciliation, not auto-failure.

Scene 6 · Guarantee Boundary

TENIR-Gov provides three tiers of guarantee. A governance decision does not, by itself, prove that an external effect occurred exactly once.

Guarantee Tiers

GuaranteeLevel
At-most-once dispatch through the governance gateLocal (in-process / single-host)
Fail-closed compositionLocal
Mandatory invariants not skippable by healthy scoreLocal
Atomic budget reservationsLocal (threading.Lock)
Cross-process serialization (single host)Local (fcntl.flock)
Cross-host budget / idempotency coordinationRequires distributed infrastructure
Exactly-once external effectsDepends on downstream provider
PyPI artifact integrityPackaging ecosystem + verification

The Precise Guarantee Boundary

A governance decision does not, by itself, prove that an external effect occurred exactly once. TENIR-Gov guarantees that at most one dispatch is permitted through the governance gate on a single host.

Exactly-once external effects require provider-side idempotency (e.g., Stripe's Idempotency-Key header) and, for multi-host deployments, distributed coordination infrastructure.

Fail-closed is the default: when either kernel errors, when data is missing, or when the outcome is indeterminate, the composition produces HOLD — never PASS.

What This Means in Practice

Single host
TENIR provides at-most-once dispatch. The governance gate will not permit the same operation to dispatch twice.
Multi-host
Replace the file-backed registry with a transactional database. The in-process budget coordinator needs a distributed lock service.
External effects
The provider must support idempotency keys (e.g., Stripe). TENIR passes the governance-assigned key; the provider deduplicates on its side.
Uncertainty
Timeouts produce UNKNOWN, not FAILED. Reconciliation asks the provider what happened. If still unknown, a human reviews.
